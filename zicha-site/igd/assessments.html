Assessment

Six components make up the final grade. Quizzes, letters and the midterm are written in class; the synthetic reflection essay is submitted through Brightspace; the final discussion is in person during reading week.

Completion requirement. Every assessment component must be submitted or attended in order to pass this course, even where what you produce is incomplete. An unfinished essay handed in is a grade; an essay not handed in is a fail. The dropped-lowest allowance on the quizzes and the letters is built into how those components are scored and is unaffected by this rule.

Quizzes · 20%

Start of session 1, weeks 2–6, no more than 15 minutes. Any minute you are late is a minute you do not have. They test how carefully you read the assigned North chapters for key concepts, and usually one item asks you to integrate those concepts in a short written answer. Lowest dropped, including one missed through absence.

Letters · 25%

Start of session 2, weeks 1–3 and 5–7: 10–15 minutes, half a side of A4, handwritten. Not a summary and not a test answer — the integrative thought you had while reading: a connection between two texts, a tension you could not resolve, an objection, a case the argument reminded you of. Every letter gets an individual written reply with the grade on it. No make-up: the dropped lowest is the accommodation. No rubric, deliberately. The three strongest letters each week may be posted as models; the strongest earns a bonus on the midterm.

Midterm essay · 20%

Thursday 19 November, first hour of session 2. The prompt is handed out at 13:15 and time expires 60 minutes later. One well-defined prompt on the core concepts of the course; you receive elaborated written feedback. Extra time or an alternative sitting must be arranged with the instructor in advance.

Synthetic reflection essay · 15%

Take-home, at most 1,500 words, two connected tasks: an account of what you have learned and how it has affected your thinking, grounded in a case — a governance or development situation looked at through the course’s concepts. Not a research paper; enough to show the framework doing real work on something concrete. The case need not be grand: your student association will serve as well as an Ebola outbreak. Roughly a third of the mark rests on the case. Prompt distributed Thursday 10 December; due Sunday 13 December, 23:59, on Brightspace.

Final individual discussion · 10%

Reading week, after your essay is in. A conversation about the case, not a defence: why this case, what the institutional framing brought into view, where the account strains against the evidence, what you would do differently with more room. It is also where the substantive feedback on your essay is delivered. Graded on the discussion itself: a thin essay discussed well earns the credit; a strong essay its author cannot talk about does not.

Participation · 10%

Per day: 0 did not participate · 0.5 contributed nothing original · 1 original contribution · 1.5 repeated and enthusiastic. Aim for at least ten active discussions. Coming unprepared costs 25% of the participation grade; four instances is an F. Questions and discussion on Brightspace, games and activities earn bonus points.

Policies in brief

Attendance. Two absences are permitted. An unexcused absence costs one participation point unless you submit a 2–3 page integrative essay on the missed session’s readings within a week (pass/fail). Missing a session 2 in a reflection week also forfeits that letter; the dropped-lowest rule absorbs the first.

Late work is usually not accepted. Extensions require documented extenuating circumstances over a substantial period before the deadline, or evidence of substantial progress on the assignment. Start early.

Grading. Objective components (quizzes, participation) are scored 1–10 and converted to letters at the end of the block; written work is graded A–F on the LUC e-Prospectus descriptors; the final grade is the weighted average of grade points across components. The full text, with the conversion table, is in the syllabus.
