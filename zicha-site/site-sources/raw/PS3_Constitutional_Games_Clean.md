### Question 1: Why Prisoner's Dilemmas Need External Enforcement

[READ] In a prisoner's dilemma, both players have dominant strategies that lead to mutual harm. Consider two neighboring towns deciding whether to maintain a shared bridge:

                    Town B

                Maintain    Don't Maintain

Town A: Maintain   (3,3)       (-2,4)

   Don't Maintain  (4,-2)      (-1,-1)

**Understanding the Payoffs:**

- **Both Maintain (3,3)**: Each town pays 5 units in maintenance costs but gets 8 units of bridge benefit = net 3

- **One Maintains, Other Doesn't (-2,4)**:

- Maintainer: Pays full 5 units cost, gets only partial bridge benefit of 3 units = -2

- Non-maintainer: Pays nothing, gets partial bridge benefit of 4 units = 4

- **Neither Maintains (-1,-1)**: Bridge deteriorates, each loses 1 unit from lack of access

- This creates the prisoner's dilemma: not maintaining is always individually better, but mutual maintenance is collectively optimal

### Question 4: Sovereign vs. Contracted Constitutions

[READ] **Hardin's Framework**:

- **Sovereign constitutions** (nations, UN): No external enforcer. Stability comes from coordination - everyone follows because everyone expects everyone else to follow. Failure means chaos/civil war.

- **Contracted constitutions** (corporations, clubs): Courts can enforce rules. Stability comes from legal remedies, BUT firms still fail through poor decisions or market forces.

Key difference: Sovereign constitutions can only be replaced through coordination on a new equilibrium. Contracted ones can be enforced by courts but may still collapse if the underlying business fails.

### Question 11: Bureaucratic Pathology - Why Agencies Grow and Never Shrink

[READ] Niskanen observed that bureaucracies systematically oversupply their services and grow beyond efficient size. Consider the Department of Agricultural Support, created in 1950 when 20% of citizens were farmers. Today, 2% are farmers, but the department has tripled in size and now runs urban nutrition programs, suburban lawn care advisories, and restaurant inspection protocols.

**Niskanen's Core Logic - Quick Version:**

- Agencies know: True minimum cost = X

- Congress knows: Only what agency reports

- Agency claims: "We need 2X to do the job properly"

- Congress thinks: "Maybe they need 1.5X, let's give them 1.8X"

- Result: Agency gets 1.8X for job costing X = 80% waste

The budget game works like this: The agency knows its true minimum cost to provide services but Congress doesn't. The agency requests the maximum budget Congress might approve. Congress can't verify if the budget is justified. Once established, programs create constituencies who lobby for continuation.
